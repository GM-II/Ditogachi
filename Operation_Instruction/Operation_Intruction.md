# Operation Instructions

This section describes the standard operating procedure used to perform quasi-static tensile experiments and synchronized image-based deformation measurements using the **Potrómetro**, our proposed Open-Hardware development.

## Initial Conditions and System Preparation

All experiments were performed at room temperature under quasi-static loading conditions. Prior to each test, the system was inspected to ensure correct electrical connections (for detailed information on the electronic assembly, see the *Building Instructions* file), mechanical alignment, and proper communication between hardware and software components.

Before performing the test, the sample was marked with a pattern of dots aligned with its geometric center. The sample was then mounted between the mechanical grips and aligned with the vertical loading axis.

The macro camera was positioned at an inclination angle between 10° and 20° relative to the sample surface to ensure an adequate field of view, while the micro camera was positioned perpendicular to the sample. Both cameras were connected to the acquisition computer using USB 3.0 connections. Camera focus and illumination were adjusted manually before starting each test.

## Hardware Configuration

The linear actuation system consisted of a lead screw with a pitch of 5 mm/rev, driven by a closed-loop NEMA 23 servo/stepper motor (Rtelligent 57AM30ED or equivalent), controlled through a T60 driver configured at 40,000 microsteps. Under this configuration, a pulse frequency of 300 Hz resulted in a displacement rate of approximately 0.05 mm/s, ensuring quasi-static loading conditions. The electronic components mentioned are specified in the *Building Instructions* and *Bill of Materials*.

Force measurements were acquired using a load cell instrumented with strain gauges and connected to an Arduino Nano microcontroller. Motor control signals were generated by an Arduino Uno, which output a pulse-width modulated (PWM) signal with configurable frequency. Both microcontrollers communicated with the acquisition computer through serial communication at a baud rate of 115200.

## Operational Sequence

1. Mount and align the sample between the grips (see Fig. 3).
2. Focus and align the cameras according to the macro and micro configurations using the proprietary software provided by the camera manufacturer. If a different camera is used, this procedure can be performed using the computer’s camera application.
3. Launch the Python acquisition software and establish serial communication with both Arduino boards.
4. Activate the motor by sending a pulse signal at the predefined frequency.
5. Manually select the region of interest (ROI) corresponding to the sample area to be tracked (see Fig. 4).
6. Acquire force data and image frames synchronously during the loading process.
7. If pausing the experiment is required, press the `p` key to temporarily halt data acquisition. Press the same key again to resume the experiment.
8. Terminate the experiment manually by pressing the `q` key once the desired deformation level or failure condition has been reached.

## Data Acquisition and Synchronization

Force measurements were acquired through the Arduino Nano by reading amplified voltage signals from the load cell and converting them into force values using previously established calibration parameters. The Python software requested force measurements on demand and stored them synchronously with image acquisition.

Image acquisition was performed using OpenCV. Two acquisition modes were implemented:

1. **Image capture for post-processing analysis**
2. **Real-time deformation analysis**

In both modes, image frames from the macro and micro cameras were captured within a single acquisition loop to avoid data desynchronization and frame-rate instability.

In post-processing mode, each captured frame was associated with a corresponding force measurement. In real-time mode, characteristic points were detected using the ORB (*Oriented FAST and Rotated BRIEF*) algorithm, and frame-to-frame feature matching was performed. Mean displacements along the x and y axes, as well as a global displacement metric, were computed using NumPy and stored together with force data.

## Calibration Procedure

Prior to experimental testing, the load cell was calibrated using known reference loads to establish the voltage-to-force conversion relationship.

Camera-based displacement measurements relied on the known optical parameters of the macro and micro imaging systems, including field of view and magnification.

Calibration procedures were repeated whenever hardware components were reassembled or camera configurations were modified.

## Output Data and Post-Processing

Force data and associated displacement measurements were stored in spreadsheet format using OpenPyXL. Image frames from both cameras were saved to disk during acquisition.

After completion of the experiment, video files were generated from the recorded frames using FFmpeg via terminal commands.

This acquisition strategy was adopted to ensure temporal consistency between force and image data and to avoid issues such as irregular frame rates or data loss observed during preliminary testing.
